# Techniques glossary

The exact techniques the rubric's checks refer to, so reviewers apply them the same way every time. Sources were
checked against the books and the authors' own sites (Sep 2026); anything that is OneAway's own rule is labelled so.

| Technique | What it is | On a transcript | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Up-front contract** | Agreement at the start on purpose, time, their agenda, our agenda, and the outcome, including that "no" is fine ("think it over" is not an outcome) | First 3–6 turns of ours | Sandler, *You Can't Teach a Kid to Ride a Bike at a Seminar* |
| **Pain funnel** | 8 follow-ups that take a problem from surface to cost to feeling: tell me more · an example? · how long? · what have you tried? · did it work? · what has it cost you? · how do you feel about it? · have you given up on it? | Follow-up questions right after they name a problem | Sandler pain funnel |
| **Don't spill your candy in the lobby** | Don't present capabilities before the problem is found | Capability talk before any problem is named | Sandler rule |
| **Current state / root cause** | Keenan's current state: environment, problems, impact, **root cause**, emotion. Without the root cause the solution is a guess | The prospect explains *why*, or confirms our "why" | Keenan, *Gap Selling* |
| **Diagnose before prescribing** | Don't accept the buyer's self-diagnosis unexamined; don't prescribe until diagnosed | "You may be right; let's check" | Enns, *Win Without Pitching Manifesto*, proclamation 3 |
| **Implication question** | Explores the consequence of a problem they already named | "If reps book one meeting a week, what does that do to Q4?" | Rackham, *SPIN Selling* |
| **Need-payoff question** | Gets *them* to say the value of fixing it | "If you had 30 meetings a month, what would that mean for the plan?" A leading pitch ("wouldn't it be great if…") is not one | Rackham |
| **Conceptual agreement** | Before any proposal, agree objectives (outcomes, not deliverables), how success is measured, and what it's worth | Quantify; a tip on proposal calls | Weiss, *Million Dollar Consulting* |
| **Label** | Name their feeling or position: "It sounds like / seems like / looks like…", then stop talking | Statement about their feeling, followed by a short turn or silence from us | Voss, *Never Split the Difference* |
| **Mirror** | Repeat their last 1–3 words as a question: "Not much success?" | A short turn echoing the end of theirs | Voss |
| **Summary → "that's right"** | Paraphrase their situation until they say "that's right" ("you're right" means stop talking) | Our restatement, then their confirmation | Voss |
| **Calibrated question** | Open how/what question that makes them think; avoid "why" at their decisions | "How are you booking meetings today?" | Voss |
| **Brush-off** (the anti-pattern) | Answering a fear with reassurance or denial: "rest assured", "no, it won't" | Worry, then an immediate "don't worry" | OneAway rule, from Voss's labels |
| **Benefit vs feature** | A benefit shows how we meet a need **they stated**; a feature is a fact about us; an advantage is how it *could* help. Only benefits correlate with winning bigger deals | A tip: tie every explanation to something they said | Rackham |
| **Commercial insight** | A reframe of their situation they hadn't considered, leading to something we do better | They engage: "hadn't thought of that" | Dixon & Adamson, *The Challenger Sale* |
| **Unapplied insight vs free work** | Patterns we see across clients are fine to share; building their plan (ICP, sequence, copy) on the call is giving away the paid work | teach.insight | Baker, *The Business of Expertise*; Enns proclamation 8 |
| **Money early** | Raise money on the first call, after value; Gong: first-call price talk wins more, ideally ~¾ of the way in | price.plain on call 1 | Enns proclamation 9; Gong (11,331 deals) |
| **Options** | Two or three options rising in scope and price, highest first ("a choice of yeses") | A tip on proposal calls | Enns, *Pricing Creativity*; Weiss |
| **Price moves with scope** | Lower price only by removing scope ("what would you like me to remove?"), never a discount for nothing | price.held | Weiss, *Value-Based Fees* |
| **Sales story** | Answer "what do you do?" in three parts: their problems → what we do → why us. Lead with their issues, keep the offer short, never a feature dump | offer.stated (the shape, whatever the offer) | Weinberg, *New Sales. Simplified.* |
| **Advance vs continuation** | An advance is a specific commitment that moves the sale (a booked meeting with purpose and people). A continuation is a pleasant ending with nothing specific ("drop me some options") | close.booked | Rackham |
| **Post-sell** | Test the commitment: "What could get in the way before Thursday?" | A tip after close.booked | Sandler |
| **Trust equation** | Trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) ÷ self-orientation; neediness and ego lines cost trust | Coaching notes (not the claims check) | Maister, *The Trusted Advisor* |

Not scorable from a transcript, so never scored: tone of voice, pauses and silence, body language, interruptions
(Grain merges overlapping speech), what was on screen.
